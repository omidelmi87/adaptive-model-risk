# Research Questions

The Adaptive Model Risk project investigates how predictive models behave when the environment in which they operate changes.

The initial research agenda is organized around five primary questions.

## RQ1 — Early Detection

**How early can statistical changes in model inputs or outputs identify subsequent deterioration in predictive performance?**

This question examines whether distribution-shift indicators can function as early-warning signals before substantial model-performance degradation becomes observable.

## RQ2 — Consequential vs. Benign Drift

**Which types of distribution shift are materially associated with deterioration in model discrimination, calibration, or prediction error?**

Not every statistically significant change in the data creates meaningful model risk.

This research question examines whether different drift measures can distinguish between benign population changes and changes that materially affect model reliability.

## RQ3 — Model-Aware Drift

**Can drift measures that incorporate model sensitivity or feature importance better identify consequential changes than distribution-based measures alone?**

This question explores whether monitoring can account for the importance of individual variables or relationships to the predictive model rather than treating all observed changes equally.

## RQ4 — Regime and Stress Analysis

**Can historical economic regimes or controlled stress scenarios identify conditions under which a previously validated model becomes unreliable?**

The project will examine model behavior across different economic periods and may introduce controlled changes in data distributions or relationships to evaluate model sensitivity.

## RQ5 — Model Intervention

**How can multiple monitoring signals be combined to inform model-management responses?**

Potential responses include:

- continued monitoring;
- investigation;
- recalibration;
- threshold adjustment;
- partial retraining;
- full retraining;
- model redevelopment.

The objective is not to automate institutional model-governance decisions, but to investigate whether monitoring evidence can support more informative diagnosis and decision-making.
